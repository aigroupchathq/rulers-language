Tone shapes the conversation, not the truth. A warm or confident voice can make an answer easier to hear. It cannot make unsupported claims reliable. Keep your judgement in the loop.

      04 / Learn from real projects

      Turn a vague ask
      into useful direction.

      I use these four imagined Datum situations to show how I would brief an agent. They are teaching examples, not stories of work that has already succeeded.

      About Datum: Datum is a hypothetical vehicle platform in these lessons. Treat each prompt as a starting point to adapt, not as evidence of completed work or proven results.

      DESIGN / CREATIVE DIRECTION

      Explore a distinctive interface without breaking the architecture.

      Situation

      Imagine you are improving vehicle discovery in Datum. You want the experience to feel distinctive without unsettling the routes, data flow, or features people already rely on.

      Vague request: “Make Datum feel bold, premium, and unlike anything else.”

      Improved, copy-ready prompt

      InstructionWhy it matters
      Inspect existing tokens, routes, components and data flow.Grounds creative work in the actual project instead of inventing a replacement system.
      Show two concrete directions and their trade offs.Turns “distinctive” from a vague adjective into choices people can compare.
      Preserve architecture and stop before editing.Keeps this exploration safe and separate from implementation.

      Expected evidence Two visible concepts, a recommendation, stated assumptions and confirmation that no files changed.

      Common failure A generic “premium” facelift that silently replaces working navigation or adds unrelated features.

      Try it Choose one existing screen. Ask for two different treatments, then name one familiar element each must preserve.

      IMPLEMENTATION / INTERFACE CONSISTENCY

      Check the screens people reach after the polished first view.

      Situation

      Imagine the main vehicle page looks polished, while less visible screens and nested dialogs may use different patterns or lack useful states. This is a question to inspect, not a known Datum defect.

      Vague request: “Make the whole app feel consistent.”

      Improved, copy-ready prompt

      InstructionWhy it matters
      Inventory secondary screens and nested dialogs first.Prevents the agent from judging consistency only by the landing screen.
      Check loading, empty, error and disabled states.Those states are part of the interface, even when the happy path looks finished.
      Reuse existing tokens and report checks actually run.Reduces visual drift and shows which states were observed rather than assumed.

      Expected evidence A screen/state inventory, a list of specific changes, and test or preview results for touched flows.

      Common failure Repainting the main screen while leaving nested dialogs, empty results or disabled controls inconsistent.

      Try it Choose one secondary flow. List its screens, nested dialogs, and four states before asking for one focused improvement.

      VERIFICATION / SYSTEM EVIDENCE

      Separate unanswered questions from risks and real defects.

      Situation

      Imagine someone reports that a vehicle filter or dialog may be behaving incorrectly. The concern deserves attention, but it is not yet a confirmed defect.

      Vague request: “Audit the system and tell me what’s broken.”

      Improved, copy-ready prompt

      InstructionWhy it matters
      Label questions, suspected risks, and confirmed defects separately.Keeps uncertainty from being mistaken for a proven failure.
      Require reproduction steps and observed versus expected results.Gives another person a way to check the claim.
      Mark unchecked work inconclusive and do not repair it.Preserves verification as an independent account of the current system.

      Expected evidence A classification for each item, reproduction steps and concrete observations for confirmed defects, and explicit gaps.

      Common failure Listing every concern as a vulnerability or defect without running a check.

      Try it Write one open question and one suspected risk about a vehicle filter. Decide what you would need to observe before calling either one a defect.

      DESIGN / EXPLORATORY COMMERCIAL RESEARCH

      Research problems and alternatives without manufacturing demand.

      Situation

      Before investing in a vehicle platform, you want to understand the problem people face, how they handle it today, and what might make a new solution unnecessary.

      Vague request: “Prove there is a huge market for Datum and show why it will win.”

      Improved, copy-ready prompt

      InstructionWhy it matters
      Investigate customer problems, workarounds, and alternatives.Starts with what people need today rather than assuming the proposed product is needed.
      Look for contrary evidence and cite verifiable sources.Reduces confirmation bias and makes claims traceable.
      Call conclusions hypotheses, not validated demand.Desk research cannot substitute for direct evidence from intended customers.

      Expected evidence Dated sources, current alternatives, counter-evidence, separated facts and interpretations, and questions for later customer research.

      Common failure Invented market-size figures or confident demand claims based on a few search results.

      Try it Find one source that supports and one that challenges a vehicle buyer problem hypothesis. Write down what neither source can tell you.

      Research can suggest what to test next. It cannot confirm demand. Treat these findings as hypotheses until suitable research with prospective customers provides stronger evidence.

      ITERATION

      A small, useful glossary

      Know enough
      to make good calls.

      I do not expect you to become an engineer before you start. Learn these few terms so you can follow what the AI is building and ask better questions.

      Frontend

      The part of a website or app a person sees and uses, such as pages, buttons, forms and menus.

      Backend

      The behind-the-scenes logic that handles tasks such as accounts, permissions, payments or saving information.

      Database

      An organised place where an application stores information. Decide what it needs to store, who can access it and when it should be deleted.

      API

      A defined way for one piece of software to ask another for information or request an action. Treat API keys like passwords and never publish them in front-end code.

      Git and GitHub

      Git records changes to project files so you can review or return to an earlier version. GitHub stores and shares that history online. A project is not backed up to GitHub until you push it there.

      Environment variable

      A configuration value supplied outside the public source files. It is often used for keys and settings, but it still needs correct access controls and should not be exposed to a browser.

      Deploy

      Publish a version of your project so other people can open it. A successful deploy means the files were published, not that every feature is secure, correct or ready for production.

      07 / Honest answers

      Good questions
      to ask early.

      These are questions I hear often. I will give you the honest version, including where AI is useful and where you still need to check its work.

      Do I need to know how to code?

      No. You can begin by describing a small project in plain language. Learning a few basics over time will help you spot mistakes, understand trade-offs and maintain what you build.

      Can AI build a production-ready app for me?

      AI can help make a prototype or parts of an application, but generated code still needs review and testing. Authentication, payments, personal data and other sensitive features need particular care. Do not launch something critical just because it runs in a preview.

      Which tool should I choose?

      Choose based on what you want to make, how much control you need and what you are comfortable learning. Try a small project first, check export options and review the current plan limits before you invest time or money.

      How do I keep my work if I stop using a builder?

      Check whether the platform lets you export or sync the project to GitHub. Download a copy you can access, and learn how to open it outside the original tool before your project becomes difficult to move.

      Is this guide connected to the tools listed?

      This is an independent learning guide. Tool names and links are provided to help you explore. Features and plans may change, so confirm details on each provider’s official site.
